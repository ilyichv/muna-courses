# How LLMs work

How large language models work under the hood, from the math up: neural nets, the transformer, pretraining, inference, and adapting a model with fine-tuning, LoRA and reinforcement learning. For programmers with no neural-net background and rusty linear algebra who want to train and fine-tune models themselves. The course teaches the ideas; the coding happens outside the app, for example alongside Karpathy's lectures.

## Units
1. **Vectors and dot products** (`vectors`): vectors as lists of numbers and as arrows, adding and scaling, length, the dot product as a measure of alignment, cosine similarity, and vectors with hundreds of dimensions as a first look at embeddings. Sources: Essence of linear algebra chapters 1, 2 and 9.
2. **Matrices** (`matrices`): a matrix as a grid and as a transformation, matrix times vector as many dot products, matrix times matrix, the shape rule, transposes, non-square matrices that change dimension, rank and low-rank products, rotations, and tensors with a batch dimension. Sources: Essence of linear algebra chapters 3, 4, 7 and 8; makemore part 1 (tensors and broadcasting).
3. **Derivatives and the chain rule** (`derivatives`): the derivative as a slope you can estimate by nudging the input, partial derivatives, the gradient as the uphill direction, and the chain rule for functions inside functions. Sources: Essence of calculus chapters 1 to 4; micrograd.
4. **Probability, softmax and cross-entropy** (`probability`): probability distributions over a vocabulary, exp and log, logits, softmax, sampling, likelihood and negative log likelihood, cross-entropy as average surprise, and perplexity. Sources: makemore part 1; 3Blue1Brown's cross-entropy video; Essence of calculus chapter 5.
5. **Neurons and layers** (`neurons`): a neuron as a weighted sum plus a nonlinearity, why stacked linear layers collapse into one, activation functions (tanh, ReLU, GELU), layers as matrix multiplications, the multilayer perceptron, the forward pass, and counting parameters. Sources: 3Blue1Brown neural networks chapter 1; micrograd; makemore part 2.
6. **Gradient descent and backpropagation** (`backprop`): loss functions, gradient descent and the learning rate, the computational graph, backpropagation as the chain rule run backwards, gradients that add up, and autograd in PyTorch. Sources: 3Blue1Brown neural networks chapters 2 to 4; micrograd; makemore part 4.
7. **Training well** (`training`): minibatches, train, validation and test splits, overfitting, initialization and saturated activations, batch and layer normalization, momentum, Adam and AdamW, learning-rate warmup and decay, and reading loss curves. Sources: makemore parts 2 and 3; the Adam, AdamW and Layer Normalization papers.
8. **Predicting the next token** (`next-token`): language modelling as next-token prediction, a bigram model from counts, sampling text, scoring with negative log likelihood, a neural model over a context window, embeddings as learned lookup tables, and base models that just continue text. Sources: makemore parts 1 and 2; Bengio et al., "A Neural Probabilistic Language Model" (2003); 3Blue1Brown, "Large Language Models explained briefly".
9. **Tokenization** (`tokenization`): why not characters or words, Unicode and UTF-8 bytes, byte pair encoding (training and encoding), the vocabulary size trade-off, pre-tokenization and special tokens, and the quirks tokens cause (spelling, arithmetic, other languages). Sources: "Let's build the GPT Tokenizer"; Sennrich et al., "Neural Machine Translation of Rare Words with Subword Units" (2016).
10. **Attention** (`attention`): why tokens need context, averaging the past with a triangular matrix, queries, keys and values, scaled dot-product scores, softmax weights, the causal mask, multi-head attention, and why cost grows with the square of the context. Sources: "Let's build GPT"; 3Blue1Brown neural networks chapter 6; Vaswani et al., "Attention Is All You Need" (2017).
11. **The transformer block** (`transformer`): token and position embeddings, attention plus MLP in a block, residual connections, layer norm before each part, stacking blocks, unembedding to logits, weight tying, and GPT-2's sizes and parameter count. Sources: "Let's build GPT" and "Let's reproduce GPT-2 (124M)"; 3Blue1Brown neural networks chapters 5 and 7; the GPT-2 paper, "Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners" (2019).
12. **Modern architectures** (`modern-models`): what changed since GPT-2: rotary position embeddings (RoPE), RMSNorm, SwiGLU, grouped-query attention, mixture of experts (routers, active and total parameters), and long context. Sources: the LLaMA, RoFormer, GQA, "GLU Variants Improve Transformer" and Mixtral papers; CS336 lectures 3 and 4; nanochat.
13. **Pretraining data** (`data`): where the text comes from (Common Crawl), extracting text from HTML, language and quality filtering, exact and near deduplication, removing personal data, data mixtures (code, math, books), synthetic data, and why quality beats quantity. Sources: the FineWeb paper (2024); "Deep Dive into LLMs like ChatGPT"; CS336 data lectures.
14. **Training at scale** (`scale`): compute as about 6 × parameters × tokens, GPU memory and bandwidth, number formats (fp32, bf16, fp8) and mixed precision, the memory a training run needs, gradient accumulation, FlashAttention, and data, sharded, tensor and pipeline parallelism. Sources: "Let's reproduce GPT-2 (124M)"; nanochat; the mixed precision, ZeRO and FlashAttention papers; CS336 lectures 2 and 5 to 8.
15. **Scaling laws** (`scaling-laws`): loss as a power law in parameters, data and compute, reading log-log plots, Kaplan et al. and Chinchilla's compute-optimal ratio, why models are trained past it for cheaper inference, and predicting big runs from small ones. Sources: Kaplan et al., "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models" (2020); Hoffmann et al., "Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models" (2022); CS336 scaling lectures.
16. **Inference** (`inference`): the generation loop, greedy decoding, temperature, top-k and top-p, the KV cache and its size, prefill and decode, batching requests, quantization, and speculative decoding. Sources: nanochat's engine; CS336 inference lecture; Holtzman et al., "The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration" (2019); the speculative decoding paper.
17. **Supervised fine-tuning** (`fine-tuning`): base and assistant models, instruction and chat data, chat templates and special tokens, training only on the answer tokens, learning rates and epochs, catastrophic forgetting, classification heads, and what fine-tuning can and can't add. Sources: the InstructGPT paper (2022); "State of GPT"; "Deep Dive into LLMs like ChatGPT"; nanochat's SFT script; Hugging Face TRL docs.
18. **LoRA and QLoRA** (`lora`): the memory cost of full fine-tuning, frozen weights plus a low-rank update, rank and alpha, which matrices to adapt, merging adapters, 4-bit base models in QLoRA, and when LoRA is enough. Sources: the LoRA paper (2021); the QLoRA paper (2023); Hugging Face PEFT docs.
19. **Learning from preferences** (`preferences`): why comparing answers is easier than writing them, preference pairs, training a reward model, RLHF with PPO and a KL penalty, reward hacking, DPO without a reward model, and AI feedback. Sources: the InstructGPT paper; Rafailov et al., "Direct Preference Optimization" (2023); "State of GPT".
20. **RL for reasoning** (`reasoning`): chain of thought and why thinking tokens help, verifiable rewards (math answers, unit tests), the policy-gradient idea, GRPO, DeepSeek-R1, spending more compute at test time, and distilling reasoning into small models. Sources: the DeepSeek-R1 paper (2025); the DeepSeekMath paper (GRPO, 2024); "Deep Dive into LLMs like ChatGPT"; nanochat's RL script.
21. **Evaluation** (`evaluation`): bits per byte and perplexity for base models, benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) and how they're scored, contamination, LLM-as-a-judge and its biases, preference arenas, and checking your own fine-tune for regressions. Sources: the MMLU, GSM8K and HumanEval papers; "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" (2023); nanochat's evals; CS336 evaluation lecture.
